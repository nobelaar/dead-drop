# Overview — DEAD DROP: BLACKOUT

**Status:** Provisional narrative canon (direction document)  
**Document type:** Campaign premise / design direction  
**Priority:** High

> Defines overall direction, not every fact in the universe. Open questions stay open until answered in separate docs.

---

## Logline

**DEAD DROP: BLACKOUT** is a narrative FPS with a realistic tone, set during a global crisis that destabilizes the electrical systems, communications, and infrastructure modern society depends on.

The story follows **Nicolás “Nico” Ferreyra**, a cybersecurity specialist who is at work when seemingly disconnected failures begin. What looks like a rough workday becomes a crisis beyond any incident he has known.

Communications break, services fail, and reliable information grows scarce. Institutions try to hold control; people look for answers; competing interests, contradictory accounts, and opportunities for those who can exploit chaos appear.

Nico is not a secret agent, a revolutionary leader, or someone who already knows a conspiracy. He is an ordinary person with useful technical knowledge, limited resources, and personal bonds that matter more and more.

His first goal is to survive the early hours and find his friends. As the story advances he must decide whom to trust, what information to share, what risks to take, and how far he will go to protect the people he cares about.

---

## Tone

- Tech / political thriller
- Contemporary, material, recognizable setting
- Plausible technology (dependencies, tools, incomplete or misleading info)
- No zombies, no supernatural elements
- Violence has consequences; compassion can cost resources and still build something after collapse
- Not a political cartoon: corruption, abuse, propaganda, negligence, and opportunists can exist, but motives and capabilities are developed in fiction — not assumed true because of a given ideology
- The game does not claim every institution is evil or every authority is legitimate; the player weighs evidence, motives, and cost

---

## The world and the crisis

The crisis is **not** a simple conventional blackout. It is a sequence of failures affecting, in different regions and moments, electrical systems, communication networks, digital services, and institutional coordination.

Interdependence causes cascade effects: broken communications hinder emergency coordination; supply problems hit essential services; lack of information feeds rumor, fear, and contradictory decisions.

**Global scope, uneven consequences.** Some regions lose services earlier. Some systems work intermittently; others die. Existing infrastructure, local decisions, and available resources shape each community’s response.

Not everything vanishes at once. Generators, local nets, radio gear, isolated systems, and offline copies of information may remain. Useful — not magic: they need energy, maintenance, range, and people who can use them.

The crisis does not automatically turn everyone into an enemy. Some help; some cling to normalcy; some prioritize their own; some exploit the situation for power or resources.

### Truth behind the collapse

**Exact cause is not defined in this document.**

The story should allow plausible explanations, incomplete information, and interested versions of events. Characters may find signs of negligence, technical vulnerabilities, questionable political decisions, and actors who benefit from the situation — without every discovery confirming a single theory.

Some groups may have anticipated the crisis, aggravated it, or used the collapse for goals they could not push before.

**Benefiting from a catastrophe does not prove causing it.** Distinguish verifiable facts, interpretation, and propaganda.

The central mystery need not be only “who did what.” It can also involve decisions before and during the crisis, hidden information, systems nobody bothered to protect, and people who must answer for consequences.

The definitive explanation must fit observed events, involved actors’ technical capabilities, and world limits. **No supernatural fix; no omnipotent technology** to resolve conflicts.

## Protagonist

Full sheet: [../characters/nicolas-ferreyra.md](../characters/nicolas-ferreyra.md)

- Cybersecurity specialist: systems, networks, services, vulnerabilities; pattern recognition, logs, investigation, access when means exist.
- Not a universal expert (power plants, advanced fabrication, serious medicine, armed groups without cost).
- Core non-technical conflict: deep empathy; hard to abandon someone who needs help — risks, spent resources, changed plans.
- Arc is **not** learning cruelty. It is keeping humanity when helping one person endangers another, when resources do not cover everyone, and when doing the right thing does not always yield a good outcome.

## Narrative conflict (structure)

1. **Concrete start:** a workday while systems begin to fail.
2. **Expansion:** from technical incidents to material and human consequences of collapse.
3. **Harder goals:** find friends, get reliable information, decide whom to trust.
4. **Larger actors:** crisis is interpreted and exploited by groups with different interests — restore services; keep order at any cost; fill power vacuum on their terms.

Conflict is **not** a simple good faction vs bad faction. Organizations can hold mixed motives; solutions can have unintended consequences; information may be too thin for certainty.

Nico need not become the hero of a world revolution. Narrative weight comes from his decisions, who they affect, and what information he gathers.

## Player experience

Narrative FPS focused on exploration, tension, investigation, and choice.

Approaches (context-dependent; not always available; not always equivalent):

| Approach | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Technical** | Investigate systems, read logs, use terminals, plausible vulnerabilities |
| **Stealth** | Avoid fights, observe, move without drawing attention |
| **Social** | Negotiate, persuade, trade information, build trust |
| **Confrontation** | Force when possible — with risks and consequences |

Intent: combat is not the universal answer; observation and decisions matter.

**Resources** exist to create narrative decisions — not to turn the campaign into a constant sim of hunger, thirst, inventory weight, and crafting.

Consequences may show later: scenes, relationships, opportunities, available information, endings. Not every consequence is announced; not every decision has a perfect solution.

---

## Campaign design principles

- Main story is **directed and compact**, not an unlimited open world.
- Decisions change aspects of the experience without requiring fully separate campaigns per path.
- Information is revealed progressively and may contradict itself.
- Characters keep autonomy and can act off-screen.
- Nico’s skills have defined limits.
- Crisis has plausible material and human consequences.
- Endings depend on actions, omissions, alliances, and accumulated choices.
- No zombies or supernatural elements.
- Survival mechanics must not displace the weight of the story.
