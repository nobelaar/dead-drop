Nico is an ordinary person with specialized technical knowledge. He is not a secret agent, a legendary hacker, a tech millionaire, or someone destined to save the world.

Before the disaster, his biggest concerns might be finishing a report, solving a work problem, paying bills, or meeting a friend. The global crisis does not begin because he uncovered a conspiracy or because someone chose him for a mission.

He was simply living his life when everything started to fail.

---

## 2. Profession and skills

Nico works in offensive security. His job is to evaluate systems, identify vulnerabilities, and demonstrate how they could be exploited within an authorized scope.

### What he knows

- Networks, protocols, and IT services
- Operating systems, terminals, and diagnostic tools
- Permissions, authentication, identity management, and access control
- Log analysis, connections, and anomalous behavior
- Attack-surface recognition and vulnerability assessment
- Exploitation of security flaws in environments he understands
- Writing technical reports and communicating risk
- Methodical investigation of software and digital infrastructure problems

His experience lets him recognize patterns, form hypotheses, and make use of certain access paths or technical weaknesses. He still needs information, tools, and time.

### Limits of his competence

Nico cannot hack any system with a few commands. He does not automatically know the architecture of infrastructure he has never examined, cannot recover information that no longer exists, and cannot magically bypass air-gapped or fully isolated systems.

By default he is also **not**:

- An expert in power generation or electrical distribution
- A doctor or paramedic
- A trained combatant
- An outdoor survival expert
- A specialist in logistics, agriculture, or heavy mechanics
- A military strategist

He can learn some of these during the campaign, lean on other people, or improvise with uncertain results. His IT knowledge may help him investigate infrastructure; it does not make him someone who can automatically repair it.

**Design principle:** his technical intelligence should open gameplay possibilities, not erase story obstacles.

## 3. Personality

Nico should not start as a fully closed personality. He has prior history, habits, preferences, and contradictions, but his character should also emerge through decisions during the campaign.

The central trait is his capacity to genuinely care about other people.

### Core trait: empathy that is hard to shut off

Nico has a huge heart. He finds it difficult to see someone suffering and simply walk past, even when helping is inconvenient, dangerous, or against his own interests.

He does not need to know someone deeply to recognize their pain. He can feel responsible for helping a person he just met, even when he rationally understands he does not have enough resources to fix every problem he encounters.

This is not absolute naivety or inability to see danger. Nico can distrust, get angry, set boundaries, and recognize when people exploit kindness. The conflict is that understanding the risk is not always enough to make him abandon someone who needs help.

His problem is not that he never knows what would be more convenient for him. Sometimes he knows exactly what he *should* do and still cannot leave the other person behind.

### An important contradiction

Nico can coldly analyze a software vulnerability and, at the same time, struggle enormously to make a cold decision when human lives are involved.

He can understand that his resources are limited, but he struggles to accept that this limitation means he will not be able to help everyone.

In a crisis, this can lead him to deviate from his goals, take unnecessary risks, reveal information, spend resources he needs, or put himself in danger to help someone.

His empathy can also generate trust, alliances, loyalties, and solidarity networks that become essential to survival.

**Kindness must not be systematically punished by the game.** Some compassionate choices will have negative consequences; others will open paths that would never have existed if Nico had acted only out of convenience.

### Do not turn him into a saint

Nico does not have to be morally perfect. He can get frustrated, make mistakes, act on impulse, hold resentment, or help someone while secretly hoping that person will like or value him back.

He can feel guilt when he does not help and exhaustion when he does. He can feel anger at injustice and wonder why it always seems to fall on him to take responsibility.

These contradictions should appear organically, not through constant explanatory monologues.

His empathy is a core trait, not the entirety of his personality.

---

## 4. Economic situation and daily life

Nico is not rich and has no financial reserve that could protect him through a prolonged crisis.

He has relatively specialized work, but that does not mean abundance. His salary covers ordinary needs with little margin for surprises.

Before the blackout he has normal modern urban amenities: electricity, internet, transport, digital payments, supermarkets, basic services. He probably takes some of that infrastructure for granted, like almost everyone.

He must **not** start the story with a bunker, an arsenal, a stash of tactical gear, or a secret stockpile of supplies.

His computer and knowledge are professional tools, not magic resources that solve a material crisis.

When services disappear, Nico faces a reality his job never prepared him for: knowing how to diagnose a system is not the same as knowing how to live without it.

## 5. Personal relationships

Nico has **five close friends**. They are people with prior, meaningful relationships — not five NPCs whose only function is to hand the protagonist quests.

Each friendship should have identity, a life of its own, needs, and priorities independent of Nico.

Some relationships may be old; others more recent. Not all friendships need the same intensity, and Nico does not need daily contact with each of them.

### Rules for developing these relationships

- The five friends must differ in personality, needs, capabilities, and how they face the crisis.
- Not all must agree with Nico or share his values.
- Each must have something to lose, their own goals, and reasons to make decisions Nico does not control.
- Some may need help; others may be in a position to help him.
- Trust must be built and can deteriorate through concrete decisions.
- Relationships must not reduce to affinity meters or characters who passively wait to be rescued.
- It is not required that all survive, stay together, or agree with the protagonist’s choices.

## 6. Family and past

For now, no detailed family history is defined. Do **not** introduce dead parents, childhood trauma, missing relatives, or family conflict as an automatic explanation of his personality.

Nico has a life before the disaster, but he does not need a secret personal tragedy to justify his humanity.

His story should come mainly from his relationships, his work, his decisions, and how he responds to the crisis.

The past can be developed when it is narratively useful, always avoiding turning every trait into the result of a conveniently designed trauma.

---

## 7. Relationship to the disaster

Nico is at work when the first failures begin.

At first he interprets the problems through his professional experience: services that stop responding, unstable connections, incomplete logs, systems behaving anomalously.

He tries to understand what is happening with the tools and information he has. The failures progressively exceed the scope of his job.

Internal communications stop working, electricity fails, and available information becomes contradictory.

Nico does **not** discover the cause of the disaster during the prologue. He also does not obtain definitive proof of a global conspiracy.

His experience lets him distinguish some symptoms and discard certain local hypotheses, but it does not give him a complete explanation.

The character must live the crisis at the same time as the player: both gradually understand that the world is going through something extraordinary, without yet knowing what caused it or what comes next.

---

## 8. Character arc

Nico’s arc is **not** going from kind person to ruthless survivor.

It is also **not** discovering that helping others is a weakness he must eliminate.

His central conflict is learning to act with compassion in a world where resources, time, and opportunities are limited.

At the start he may feel that if he *can* help someone, he is *obligated* to. The crisis forces situations where helping one person can endanger others, compromise an important goal, or consume resources he will need later.

Over time he must confront hard questions:

- What does helping mean when you cannot save everyone?
- When is taking a risk solidarity, and when is it refusal to accept your own limits?
- Can he care for others without destroying himself in the process?
- How does he tell genuine need from someone manipulating his empathy?
- Is it possible to build community without absolute responsibility for every member?
- What does he owe his friends when helping one means leaving another waiting?

There is no single correct answer for all of these. The game should let Nico answer through actions, not only dialogue.

The outcome of his arc depends on how the player balances personal goals, bonds, and principles.

He may learn to set limits without stopping being compassionate. He may also be consumed by guilt, grow more distrustful, or find a collective way to share responsibility. These possibilities should emerge from the campaign and its choices, not a predetermined destiny.

---

## 9. Representation principles

When writing scenes, dialogue, missions, and gameplay systems for Nico, respect:

1. **He is not the Chosen One.** The disaster does not happen because of him and does not revolve exclusively around him.
2. **He is not a super-hacker.** His skills have limits, need context, and can fail.
3. **He is not an action hero by default.** Combat may be possible, but it is not his initial specialty.
4. **He has a life of his own.** His concerns must not be limited to the conspiracy plot.
5. **His empathy is real.** Helping others can cost him, but it can also create positive outcomes and meaningful alliances.
6. **He is not a saint or obligatory martyr.** He can err, get angry, distrust, and learn boundaries.
7. **He does not have to be the smartest person in every room.** Others may know more, understand better, or choose wiser.
8. **He does not know the truth in advance.** His conclusions must rest on available evidence and can be wrong.
9. **His decisions need understandable consequences.** Avoid arbitrary punishment and moral dilemmas that artificially railroad the player.
10. **The character should be revealed by playing.** Avoid excessive psychological exposition and dialogue that explicitly lists all his traits.

## Conceptual summary

Nicolás Ferreyra is an Argentine red teamer, financially tight, independent, with an intimate circle of five friends. He has real cybersecurity skills, but no extraordinary abilities outside his field. His most important trait is deep empathy that makes it hard to abandon someone in danger, even when that puts him at risk. The global crisis forces him to learn how to remain compassionate when he does not have the resources to help everyone. He is not destined to save humanity: he must decide what kind of person he wants to be while trying to survive the collapse of the infrastructure that sustained his life.
